Filling Out the Online Application

This page walks through how the NYPD's online application portal itself works — logging in, moving through it, and submitting — not what to answer. For what any specific question on the application means or how to answer it, that's between you, the application's own instructions, and, if you want it, an attorney. We stick to the mechanics here.

This is not legal advice. We don't tell you what to write in response to any application question, especially ones about your background, your reasons for applying, or your understanding of the law. Answer every question truthfully, in your own words, based on your own situation.

1. Create Your Account

Before you can start, you'll need to register on the portal using your email address. Click "Register" in the upper right-hand corner of the licensing portal and follow the prompts.

Official source: NYPD Online Licensing Portal — Instructions · Last verified: August 18, 2026

2. Expect Many Sections — That's Normal

The application is broken into a large number of separate sections, and it can take a while to get through all of them in one sitting. That's expected, not a sign you're doing something wrong — read each section's instructions as you go, and take breaks if you need to.

3. You Don't Have to Finish in One Sitting

The portal keeps a running list of applications you've started but haven't finished, in a section of your account separate from ones you've already submitted. If you need to stop partway through, you can log back in later and pick up where you left off, rather than starting over.

4. Finalizing and Submitting

Once you click "Finalize and Submit," you won't be able to go back and change your answers — so it's worth reviewing everything carefully first. After submitting, you can still upload additional documents if the NYPD requests more from you, even though your answers themselves are locked in.

Official source: NYPD Online Licensing Portal — Instructions · Last verified: August 18, 2026

5. The Confidentiality Request Option

Separately from the main application, the portal has a Confidentiality Request feature, which relates to keeping certain licensing information out of public records requests. Whether this applies to your situation is a legal judgment call, not a mechanical one — read the portal's own instructions for it carefully, and if you're unsure, that's a good question for an attorney rather than a guess.

Official source: NYPD Licensing Portal — Confidentiality Request · Last verified: August 18, 2026

6. Paying the Fee

The last part of the process is paying your application fee. See the Fee Calculator for current amounts.

Have your documents and photo ready before you start — it makes the whole thing go faster.
