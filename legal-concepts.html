Legal & Safety Concepts, in Plain English

Six terms and ideas that come up constantly in the NYC firearms licensing process, explained simply. Every explanation below links to the actual official source — read this as a starting point, not a substitute for it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Laws change, and how they apply to your specific situation can vary. For advice about your situation, consult a licensed attorney. When in doubt, the official source linked in each section always controls.

License Types: Handgun License vs. Rifle/Shotgun Permit

In New York City, possessing a handgun requires a handgun license, and possessing a rifle or shotgun requires a separate rifle/shotgun permit — they are two different NYPD processes, each with its own application. Within handgun licenses, there are further distinctions (such as premises licenses, which cover keeping a handgun at a specific home or business address, and carry licenses, which allow carrying outside the home) — the exact license types and eligibility for each are defined by the NYPD License Division.

Official source: NYPD License Division — Firearms Licensing · Last verified: August 17, 2026

Eligibility & "Good Moral Character"

New York State law requires firearms license applicants to be at least 21 years old (with an exception for honorably discharged military members) and to demonstrate "good moral character" — defined in the statute as "having the essential character, temperament and judgement necessary to be entrusted with a weapon and to use it only in a manner that does not endanger oneself or others." The law also lists specific disqualifications, including felony convictions, certain other convictions, unlawful drug use, dishonorable military discharge, involuntary mental health commitments, prior license revocations, and active orders of protection.

Official source: New York Penal Law § 400.00 · Last verified: August 17, 2026

What the Application Process Generally Involves

Broadly, applying involves completing a detailed application (name, date of birth, residency, and more), submitting a recent photograph, being fingerprinted, and undergoing a background investigation. For certain license types, applicants may also need to complete a firearms safety course, sit for an in-person interview, and provide character references. Since January 2018, the NYPD has accepted applications only through its online portal — paper applications are no longer accepted. A licensing officer has up to six months to act on a completed application.

Official source: NYPD Online Licensing Portal and New York Penal Law § 400.00 · Last verified: August 17, 2026

Safe Storage Requirements

New York law requires firearms to be safely stored in two common situations: when you live with someone under 18 or someone otherwise prohibited from possessing a firearm, and whenever a firearm is left inside a vehicle. Compliant storage means a locked, tamper-resistant safe or container that can't be opened without the key, keypad, or combination — or a gun-locking device made for that weapon. Firearms left in a vehicle must be locked out of sight, with ammunition removed. Violating this law is a criminal offense.

Official source: New York Penal Law § 265.45 · Last verified: August 17, 2026

Concealed Carry & "Sensitive Locations"

New York's Concealed Carry Improvement Act (CCIA) restricts where a licensed carry holder may bring a firearm, even with a valid license — certain "sensitive locations" (such as schools, government buildings, and other specific categories) are generally off-limits regardless of licensure. The exact list of sensitive locations is defined by state law and has been the subject of ongoing legal challenges, so it's especially important to check the current official guidance rather than a summary (including this one).

Official source: New York State — Concealed Carry Law FAQ · Last verified: August 17, 2026

NYC's System Is Separate From the Rest of New York State

New York City runs its own firearms licensing system through the NYPD License Division, which is separate from the county-clerk-administered pistol permit system used in the rest of New York State. If you're an NYC resident, your license comes from and is renewed through the NYPD — not through your county clerk or the New York State Police recertification portal, which serves permit holders outside the city. Mixing these two systems up is a common and understandable point of confusion.

Official sources: NYPD License Division and NYS Police Pistol Permit Recertification (for context on the state system) · Last verified: August 17, 2026
